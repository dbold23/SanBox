Dear Dr. Ebert,

I am Daniel Sambold at CSU Monterey Bay, working with [PI NAME] on individual photo-identification of
broadnose sevengills. I have three questions that only a hexanchiform specialist can settle, and I would

We ran our method-selection experiment before touching sevengills, on a public 9,861-individual
wild-fish re-ID catalogue with PIT-tag ground truth. Two results shaped this work: individual identity
was **not** concentrated in the rigid head, and catalogue density — images per individual — mattered
more than any modelling choice we tested. So our sevengill design puts flank pattern first, with the
nares-to-gill freckle patch as the corroborating region.

Three asks, about 45 minutes in total:

1. Review a 30-point sevengill landmark schema we drafted — seven gill slits bracketed, a single
   posterior dorsal near the pelvics, no second dorsal, an evenly spaced midline chain. It is attached.

   study, and the nearest analogue we found — epaulette sharks — reports juvenile and neonate patterns

In return: the schema, its validator and our annotation tooling released openly, with acknowledgement or
