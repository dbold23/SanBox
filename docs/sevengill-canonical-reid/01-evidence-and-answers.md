### How to read this — methodology and limits

**Read this block before any row below. It bounds every claim in the document.**

- **WebFetch to essentially every scholarly host was egress-blocked for the entire scan.** arXiv, doi.org, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, dblp, PubMed/PMC, bioRxiv, openaccess.thecvf.com, NeurIPS/PMLR proceedings, Springer/Nature, ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, Wiley, PLOS, Frontiers, HuggingFace, Kaggle, Zenodo, CRAN, nitrc.org, and most university and agency PDF hosts (fao.org, noaa.gov, tugraz.at, cs.ubc.ca, cg.tuwien.ac.at, stonybrook.edu, osf.io) all refused. **Not one publisher page or PDF was fetched in this entire scan.**
- **GitHub and raw.githubusercontent.com were the only reliably fetchable primary sources.** This asymmetry is the single most important thing to know about the evidence below: **licensing, repository contents, README text, config files and shipped data manifests are strong; author lists, venues, volume/page numbers and reported accuracy figures are weak.** Where a `[PRIMARY]` row also contains a venue or a headline number, the row says explicitly which half is primary.
- **Forward-citation chasing was requested and was NEVER performed.** Semantic Scholar, Google Scholar, Crossref and OpenAlex are all blocked here. Any statement in this document of the form "nobody has cited/extended X" rests on keyword WebSearch only and must not be reported as a citation-graph result.
- **The exhaustive full-text negative has a hard time boundary.** The corpus-grep negative for Q1 covers **~2025-03 onward only**, via an arXiv mirror (39,814 unique cs.CV records with abstracts, Mar 2025 – Aug 2026; 66,948 cs.CV titles over 2.5 years for the elongate-taxa sweep), plus an **11,877-paper elasmobranch title corpus** and **3,427 full-text elasmobranch conference abstracts** (EEA 2002–2025, JMIH/AES 2005–2016, Sharks International 2022). **Anything published before ~2025-03 and outside those elasmobranch corpora was covered by keyword search only, not by full-text grep.**
- **One search channel is known-broken and its zeros were discarded.** GitHub *code* search returned 0 hits for a phrase provably present in a repository already fetched in the same session. All GitHub code-search zeros are treated as non-evidence. GitHub *repository* search passed its control (`detectron2` → 2,512 hits) and is used.
- **One gap-fill pass ran with a fully exhausted WebSearch budget** and substituted GitHub fetches. Its negative — no released canonical-surface implementation for any fish — is therefore a negative **over released code**, not over all literature. Do not let anyone upgrade it.

## Direct answers

### 1. Has canonical-surface or UV-unwrapped re-ID been applied to ANY fish or elasmobranch?

**NO.** Not once. There is no paper, preprint, dataset, or code repository in which image observations of a fish, shark, ray, or any elasmobranch are mapped onto a canonical 3D surface or a UV parameterization for the purpose of individual identification. The intersection of the three necessary ingredients — canonical-surface mapping, an elasmobranch, and an identity task — is **empty**.

**The negative is stronger than a keyword absence, and it now has a second, independent form.** *(a) At the paper level:* three adversarial refuters attacked it independently and by different routes and all failed. Refuter 1 grepped 39,814 unique arXiv cs.CV records with full abstracts (Mar 2025 – Aug 2026), 11,877 elasmobranch paper titles, and 3,427 full-text elasmobranch conference abstracts (EEA 2002–2025, JMIH/AES 2005–2016, Sharks International 2022); the cross-product of every marine term against every canonical-surface term returned **one** hit — the seed paper itself, matching only because "seal" is in it. Refuter 2 ran a 19-term canonical-surface battery × 18-term marine battery over the same corpus plus the accepted-paper lists of CVPR 2022–2025, ICCV 2023/2025, ECCV 2022/2024 and WACV 2024, and checked the ICCV 2025 Joint Workshop on Marine Vision specifically; the single marine × dense-correspondence co-occurrence was an underwater SLAM paper with no animal and no identity. Refuter 3 read the complete Wild Me algorithm↔species mapping, enumerated all 45 WildMeOrg repos, searched detectron2/DeepLabCut/SLEAP issues and PRs for any fish or aquatic mesh request — zero — and ran an elongate-taxa attack (snakes, eels, salamanders, newts, axolotls, caterpillars, worms, sea cucumbers, lampreys) across 66,948 cs.CV titles over 2.5 years, finding **no individual-ID paper of any kind**, canonical-surface or otherwise. *(b) At the implementation level, a separate pass reached the same place through code:* the complete DensePose CSE mesh registry, fetched in full, registers **13** canonical surfaces — `smpl_27554`, `chimp_5029`, `cat_5001`, `cat_7466`, `sheep_5004`, `zebra_5002`, `horse_5004`, `giraffe_5002`, `elephant_5002`, `dog_5002`, `dog_7466`, `cow_5002`, `bear_4936` — **1 human plus 12 mammals (derived by counting the fetched registry)**, no birds, no reptiles, no fish, no non-tetrapods. A-CSM's shipped part files are bird, cow, horse, sheep only. CMR, Unicorn and SMALify cover birds, cars, rigid ShapeNet classes and quadrupeds. A GitHub repository-search sweep of released fish and shark computer vision returned **only 2D work** — YOLO/DeepLabCut keypoint pipelines, whale-shark spot matching, blacktip photo-ID, species classifiers — with **no canonical surface, no dense correspondence, no 3D template and no articulated body model anywhere.**

**Both halves of that negative are bounded, and the bounds are in the methodology block.** The corpus grep covers **~2025-03 onward** only; everything earlier was keyword-searched, not full-text grepped. Forward-citation chasing was **never performed** — Semantic Scholar, Google Scholar, Crossref and OpenAlex are all egress-blocked here, so no statement in this document about who has or has not cited a work is a citation-graph result. And the implementation-level negative is a negative **over released code**, because that pass ran with an exhausted search budget. State the finding as *"no released implementation and no paper we could reach,"* never as *"no work exists."*

**One structural finding is worth more than the negative itself, and it has now been sharpened.** The previous draft said the fish literature "chose to route around" lateral bending. That is right, but the evidence for it was weak and is now much better. The salmon skin-dot paper (Sci. Rep. 11:16904, 2021; n = 328, 100%) does localise identity away from the tail, but it does so in a single passing sentence justifying an ROI choice — *"the pattern on the left (bottom part) of the fish does not deform with fish movement, while the pattern on the right (tail part) part of the fish deforms due to the tail bending"* — **not a measured ablation.** The real evidence is elsewhere and the repair pass found it: **a published evaluation of I3S (Wildlife Research 48(1):38–) found that the difference in body angle between capture and recapture photographs SIGNIFICANTLY INCREASED error rates, and that digitally correcting body bending REDUCED them — with a dedicated "I3S Straighten" pre-processing tool existing for exactly that purpose.** That is a measurement, on an elongate aquatic animal, of the effect this project is premised on. Alongside it: the whale-shark protocol restricts matching to a region posterior to the gill slits; **the only operating sevengill photo-ID programme restricts matching to the freckling between the nares and the gill slits**; the Otago effort restricts to a top-down dorsal view; Hughes & Burghardt identify great whites from a 1-D fin contour; SeaTurtleIDHeads exists because cropping to a rigid patterned part works (91.18 vs 25–62 for deformable-body sets). **The field's universal answer is *crop to the rigid part*.** It is the incumbent, it works, it is what a sevengill programme would have to beat — **and it is also the answer a sevengill most plausibly breaks**, because the speckling is spread over an elongate maximally-flexible trunk and the single dorsal is small and far posterior. That, not novelty, is the real argument for this project.

**And the honest counterweight, which the previous draft did not carry.** The two existing sevengill efforts *succeed* precisely by using the anterior head-and-gill region — **the one part of the animal lateral undulation barely deforms.** A decade of matches in La Jolla is a field-tested prior in favour of the rigid-patch arm. If the Melops head-vs-headless ablation points the same way, the two lines of evidence together are a serious argument to descope `shark-pose-3d` rather than extend it.

### 2. What 3D shape templates / scanned meshes exist for sharks and elasmobranchs?

**Artist meshes: essentially one, and it is the wrong order.** The DigitalLife3D great white the lab already uses (3,912 verts) is the only shark asset in play, and it is **Lamniformes, not Hexanchiformes** — five gill slits, two dorsal fins. A GitHub code search for `filename:shark.obj` returned nine hits, **all** game/graphics assets. Searches for shark photogrammetry and shark anatomy CT returned zero repositories each. ⚠️ **Important caveat that has not changed:** MorphoSource, oVert/openVertebrate, Phenome10K, Digimorph, Sketchfab scientific collections and Smithsonian 3D were **all network-unreachable during this scan.** Whether a *Notorynchus* or any hexanchiform mesh exists there is genuinely **unknown, not absent**. `pyMorphoSource` (MIT) exposes the exact query and settles it in an afternoon. **Do that before spending more time speculating.**

**CT/scan-derived surfaces: a physical caveat that is easy to miss.** Even in the best case, CT resolves *calcified cartilage and jaws*, and a chondrichthyan's endoskeleton is poorly radio-opaque compared to bone. **It does not give the external skin surface that carries the pigmentation the whole hypothesis depends on.** Any scanned specimen is also a dead animal, typically fixed curled, flattened or partially dissected — not in a canonical straight pose. The one genuinely relevant tool is **Unwind**, which interactively unbends and untwists a CT-scanned fish into a straight canonical frame; it is unlicensed, manual and a C++/Qt5 GUI, but the **ScanAllFish/oVert CT corpus behind it remains the most plausible source of a real hexanchiform template.**

**Statistical shape models: none exist for any elasmobranch, and the distinction matters.** The lab's mesh is **one textured surface of one individual**, with no correspondence to any other mesh and therefore **no population variance to run PCA over.** SharkSMPL's "PCA shape space" is therefore, unless the lab has data not visible here, necessarily **synthetic** — hand-authored blendshapes — rather than learned from individuals. Every public route to a real statistical shape model (ShapeWorks MIT, SlicerMorph GPA BSD-2) requires a **population** of comparable 3D surfaces, and for *N. cepedianus* there is no evidence even one exists. The only shark shape model in the CV literature is **Paskin et al.'s Kendall shape space for basking shark heads** (MIT — the only permissive shark-shape code found), and it is head/jaw skeleton only, sparse landmarks, no dense surface, no UV, and *Cetorhinus* is a lamniform.

**Two credible routes, and the gap-fill added a third that is cheaper than both.** *(1) Learn the template from 2D images.* **Cashman & Fitzgibbon's dolphin model** built a working 3D morphable model of a limbless streamlined swimmer from **32 single-view photographs, 8 basis shapes, zero 3D scans, zero GPUs**, under a permissive Ms-PL licence. **MagicPony** (verified at source) needs no template at all: `num_legs: 0` with `body_bones_mode: z_minmax_y+` yields a **pure axial 8-bone chain** — the correct first-order shark skeleton from a one-value config edit. ⚠️ Cashman is geometry-only (no texture, no UV, no identity), and MagicPony's MIT licence is undermined by vendored NVIDIA-proprietary DMTet/render files, making it effectively non-commercial. *(2) Buy real 3D on the target species.* **The Otago group already operates a calibrated stereo-BRUV rig on free-swimming sevengills with mean calibration error <1%** (Lewis et al. 2023). That is a ready-made 3D ground-truth source on the actual animal, and it is a phone call, not a research programme. *(3) — the new one — **do not build a template-from-scans at all; build the chart on the artist template and carry it by skinning.*** See Q4/Q5 and the medical section: VMTK's (s, φ) machinery needs only a mesh, runs on CPU in minutes, and is BSD-licensed. The very first experiment should be to load the existing 3,912-vertex template into 3D Slicer, run SlicerVMTK's Extract Centerline, and **look at whether a maximal-inscribed-sphere medial axis on a shark tracks the vertebral column or wanders into the fins and caudal lobe.** That is an afternoon, and it tests the premise of the entire programme before any modelling commitment.

**Licensing landscape, restated because it constrains everything:** the entire SMAL lineage — including anything that loads a SMAL `.pkl` — sits under an MPI licence that forbids redistribution **and explicitly bans training models for commercial use**, so "just retarget SMAL" is legally as well as morphologically unavailable. Cleanly reusable in this space: **VMTK (BSD)**, **BFF (MIT)**, **OptCuts (MIT)**, **libigl/SLIM (MPL-2.0)**, **detectron2/DensePose-CSE (Apache-2.0)**, **SlicerMorph/ALPACA (BSD-2)**, **ShapeWorks (MIT)**, **TubULAR (MIT)**, **Tierpsy (MIT)**, **SLEAP (BSD-3-Clause-Clear)**.

### 3. What landmark/keypoint schemes are established for shark external morphometrics?

**The FAO/Compagno tradition is not a landmark scheme, and conflating the two is the first trap — but the previous draft's own number has been deleted.** Compagno 1984 defines **named linear distances** between anatomical points, measured with calipers on a straightened landed specimen. They are not coordinates: no configuration matrix, no Procrustes fit, no shape space, no covariance structure. ⚠️ **The draft's "roughly 40–70 linear distances" is DROPPED — no source supports any count, and the precision is exactly the failure mode being repaired.** What is corroborated is only that downstream studies follow Compagno in **species-specific subsets** — one basking-shark study enumerates **27** external measurements, which is that study's subset and not a figure from Compagno. **The real standards reference is Francis (2006), *"Morphometric minefields — towards a measurement standard for chondrichthyan fishes,"* Env. Biol. Fish. 77:407–421, whose whole premise is that measurement definitions differ between workers even on the same species and that no single agreed count exists.** The structural objections stand regardless: most "pre-X lengths" are **axial projections**, not point-to-point distances; a large fraction of endpoints are ventral (nares, mouth corners, spiracle, cloaca, clasper) and never visible in free-swimming video; and the protocol presupposes a **straight body**, which a laterally-undulating sevengill violates in every frame. **Cite Compagno for nomenclature; if a number is needed for a design argument, use the lab's own Schema v2 and say plainly that the sevengill remap is a schema DECISION, not a lookup.**

**The Type I/II/III distinction is the crux, and it is unkind to the lab's existing schema.** **Type I** = juxtaposition of tissues, true homology, constrained in all directions; **Type II** = curvature maxima with a morphogenetic justification; **Type III** = extremal or constructed points, informative in one direction only and formally non-homologous — they **slide**. Classifying the 16 keypoints: **Type I** — `eye_center`, `gill_slit` (if defined as a slit origin), `pectoral_base`, `first_dorsal_base`, `second_dorsal_base` (~4–6). **Type II** — `snout_tip`, `caudal_notch` (2). **Type III** — all five `*_tip` points plus `caudal_upper_tip`, `caudal_lower_tip`, and **both** `body_midpoint_dorsal` and `body_midpoint_ventral` (8–9). **More than half the schema is formally non-homologous.** From a single lateral underwater view the reliable set is `eye_center`, `pectoral_base`, dorsal base, `caudal_notch` and the snout region — roughly 5–6 points. Poor/avoid: `anal_fin_tip` (body-occluded), `caudal_lower_tip`, `body_midpoint_ventral` (never visible), and claspers (ventral only, males only, **conditionally present** — poison for a fixed-topology template; treat as an optional annotation channel and a sex covariate, never a template joint).

**The sevengill mismatch is structural, and the worst break is at the tree root.** `second_dorsal_base` and `second_dorsal_tip` **do not exist** — hexanchiforms have one dorsal (**2 of 16 = 12.5% undefined**). `first_dorsal_base/tip` keep their names but **invert their semantics**: the single dorsal sits far posterior, over or behind the pelvics, so every learned prior at that station — PCA shape space, LBS weights, bone lengths, temporal prior — is invalid. `gill_slit` is **ambiguous across seven slits**, and `config/keypoints_16.yaml` id 3 literally reads *"Back edge of last (5th) gill slit"*; it needs at minimum `gill_slit_1_origin` and `gill_slit_7_origin`, ideally all seven, and sevengill slits extend far ventrally, nearly meeting on the throat. `snout_tip` degrades from Type II toward Type III on a broad blunt head. `caudal_lower_tip` becomes near-meaningless against a weakly developed lower lobe. `caudal_upper_tip` shifts substantially and is frequently out of frame. And **`body_midpoint_dorsal` — the ROOT of the 16-joint tree — is a Type III constructed point defined relative to a body axis that, on a laterally flexing body, is a curve changing every frame.** A noisy root propagates error to all 15 downstream joints and corrupts PA-MPJPE globally. **Tally: 2 of 16 do not exist; 1 is fundamentally ambiguous; 2 are anatomically relocated; 2 degrade badly; 2 (including the root) are ill-posed on a bending body. Roughly 9 of 16 are compromised; about 5 transfer cleanly.**

**Recommendation: do not port Schema v2 — author a hybrid, and there are now three published precedents for authoring rather than porting.** (a) A small Type I/II anchor set: snout region, eye, gill-slit-1 origin, gill-slit-7 origin, pectoral base, pelvic base, dorsal base, anal base, caudal notch, caudal upper tip (~10 points). (b) An **equally-spaced midline semilandmark chain** from snout to caudal notch — the DeepLabCut rodent convention (`spine1-4` + `tail1-3`), the fish-biomechanics convention, and what actually handles lateral bending while giving the LBS skeleton a stable, well-conditioned root and spine. **SLEAP's user-defined skeleton graph (BSD-3-Clause-Clear) supports arbitrary node count and topology, so this is authorable today.** (c) Fin tips demoted to optional Type III points used for shape only, never as skeleton joints. This resolves the proxy-channel count cleanly: 10 + N_midline + flow/edge, with N_midline a tunable hyperparameter rather than an anatomical accident. **Precedents: CzechLynx designed a 20-point species-appropriate skeleton rather than force COCO; Melops built an anatomically-defined anatomically-defined fish keypoint schema (**cardinality disputed: the dataset source tuple has 16 entries, the Scientific Data text is reported as 11 — unresolved, do not quote either number**) organised around *opercular* (gill-cover) landmarks rather than a two-dorsal body plan — same cardinality, completely different anatomy; and ml-morph, which the field might otherwise reach for, explicitly targets "semi-rigid biological structures," which is a point AGAINST transferring it to a bending trunk.**

**One honest open risk on the dense-midline plan.** No source addresses SLEAP's or DeepLabCut's accuracy on elongate animals with **many collinear, visually near-identical** body points — a known-hard case for part-affinity-style association. The lab's existing **Pose mAP50 of 0.578 on 16 distinctive landmarks** is not encouraging about what a denser, less distinctive midline schema would score. **State it as an open question; do not gloss it.**

**And the framing that matters most.** Because roughly half the sevengill identity signal sits on the **featureless flank between the Type I anchors**, the landmark scheme *cannot* be the thing that puts that pattern into a canonical frame. That region needs sliding semilandmarks (Bookstein 1997) or a learned dense embedding (CSE) or a tube chart (VMTK). ⚠️ **But note the alternative the medical literature supplies and which is cheaper than all three: ORDERED LANDMARK SEQUENCES.** The colon-registration work carries correspondence through large deformation by matching **haustral folds as an ordered sequence whose order does not change with body position.** **A sevengill's seven gill slits → single dorsal → pelvics → cloaca → caudal keel is exactly such an ordered antero-posterior sequence — and unlike Schema v2, it is FULLY DEFINED for *Notorynchus* with nothing undefined.** The extra pair of gill slits is a **gift** here, not a liability.

### 4. How do existing methods parameterize articulated bending in elongate bodies?

**Four families, and the property that decides whether a pattern survives the unwrap is isometry — which is where the previous draft was wrong.**

**(A) Joint-tree LBS** — SMPL, SMAL, SMALST, BARC/BITE, and SharkSMPL itself. Bending is 3 rotation DOF per joint; the surface is a linear blend of bone transforms. Not isometric, not area-preserving, and it collapses volume at high curvature. A 16-joint tree rooted at `body_midpoint_dorsal` allocates almost nothing to the axial chain — the verified topology parents nearly everything directly to the root, leaving **`gill_slit` (head yaw) and `caudal_notch` (tail yaw) as the only two lateral-bending DOF for the entire animal.** For a hexanchiform it is wrong twice: wrong anatomy and wrong DOF allocation. ⚠️ **And A-CSM, which the previous draft nominated as the fix, has the same disease: its shipped part files are 7 parts for a horse and 3 for a bird — a star, not a serial chain.**

**(B) Learned / implicit skinning** — BANMo, RAC, MagicPony, 3D-Fauna. Bones are discovered rather than authored; BANMo's **treeless "bag of bones"** assumes no limbs at all, so an anguilliform trunk is expressible by seeding more bones. This dissolves the "my schema doesn't transfer" problem entirely. ⚠️ Still a blend of rigid transforms, so **not isometry-constrained**; and RAC/lab4d's `get_predefined_skeleton()` hard-codes only HUMAN and QUAD, so a sevengill chain is a bounded edit to one file, not free discovery.

**(C) Geometry-processing deformation and flattening** — ARAP, BFF/OptCuts/SLIM, functional maps, and **now the entire medical tubular-flattening lineage.** This family treats near-isometry as an explicit objective. ⚠️ **But the biomechanics says the premise is false in the direction that matters most, and this is the single largest correction to the previous draft's reasoning.** The draft asserted that shark skin is "a stiff, minimally-stretching collagen composite, close to inextensible," making isometry a physically motivated prior. It is not. **Donley & Shadwick (JEB 206, 2003) measured red-muscle strain in a leopard shark at only ~1.0 BL/s as ±3.9% anterior, ±6.6% mid-body, ±4.8% posterior, in phase with local midline curvature and reproduced by a bending-beam model at all three sites.** The fish literature states the gradient explicitly: **"the greatest shortening occurring near the skin and the least shortening near the backbone."** **(derived, and labelled as such — not a figure from any paper):** since shark red muscle is superficial and beam strain scales linearly with distance from the neutral axis, **skin strain is ≥ the muscle values**, it is **antisymmetric** (the convex flank stretches while the concave flank compresses in the same frame), and for a ~0.10 m half-width at R ≈ 0.8–1.0 m it implies roughly **10–12% per flank, a ~20–25% inter-flank longitudinal scale differential at equal *s*** — a 5 cm scar reading ~5.5 cm on one side and ~4.5 cm on the other. Biology makes it worse: shark skin is a pressurized **crossed-helical collagen exotendon** that is **more extensible longitudinally than dorsoventrally — i.e. most extensible exactly in the direction *s* runs** — and its stiffness is under **active hydrostatic control varying with swim speed**, so the same shark at the same bend angle deforms differently at cruise versus burst. **A learned deformation correction therefore cannot be a pure function of pose.** ⚠️ Note also that the exotendon claim is **contested in print** and should not be cited as settled.

**(D) Centerline / rod / low-dimensional basis** — Cosserat rods, Lighthill's elongated-body theory, eigenworms, WormPose, and the real fish midline toolchain. Posture is a **1-D field along arc length**: κ(s) or θ(s). **WormPose** is the strongest precedent for this body plan: a fully anguilliform, self-occluding animal parameterized as a tangent-angle profile with **no joint tree**, trained from **500,000 purely synthetic renders with zero human keypoint labels** — validating the lab's Blender pipeline for a species with no annotated corpus. **Concrete DOF numbers, now properly sourced:** **four eigenworm modes capture >95% of *C. elegans* posture variance** (Stephens et al. 2008, PLoS Comput. Biol. 4:e1000028, corroborated independently by Broekmans et al. 2016, eLife 5:e17227) — and the **fish analogue exists: three PCs explain >90% of teleost larval tail-shape variance, with PC1 = "turniness" and PC2/PC3 = tail oscillation.** ⚠️ **The previous draft's "Kinemetrix … 200 evenly spaced stations" is deleted — that tool does not exist.** The real toolchain is **trackter** (C. Kenaley, CRAN v0.1.1, GPL ≥2), which fits a smoothed midline per frame; published use extracts on the order of **200 midline x/y stations per frame** — notably in **Di Santo et al., PNAS 2021**, whose Dryad deposit contains exactly that structure — and derives **amplitude envelope** (maximum lateral displacement from the axis of progression) and **propulsive wavelength** (distance between internodes where the smoothed midline crosses the wave-progression axis). **"Curvature per body length" is dropped as an unverified output, and 200 is not a documented hard default.** ⚠️ Retained correction: **Cosserat rods are extensible and shearable by definition** — arc-length preservation is a stiffness choice, not a guarantee.

**How many axial DOF does a sevengill actually need? Nobody knows, and the gap is now precisely characterised.** Across 22 searches there is **no measured swimming kinematics for *Notorynchus cepedianus*, or for ANY hexanchid** — no amplitude envelope, no propulsive wavelength, no maximum curvature. The claim "sevengills are more anguilliform than lamnids" is defensible on body form and **must be written as a hedged morphological expectation with no number attached.** The honest construction is a **bracket**: subcarangiform sharks below (Webb & Keyes 1982; Donley & Shadwick 2003; Berio et al. 2025 on *Scyliorhinus canicula*, the closest measured analogue), anguilliform elongate vertebrates above (Gillis 1997 on *Siren intermedia*; the **eel figure of 0.58 BL propulsive wavelength vs ~1.0 for trout/mackerel** in Di Santo et al. 2021; and the Tytell lab's **released lamprey midline data**, which is the only realistic route to a real anguilliform bending corpus). **The 0.58-vs-1.0 ratio is the concrete quantity that should set the number of bending DOF: an eel-like swimmer fits nearly two wave-cycles on its body where a carangiform fits one.** A **dem-bones/SSDR sweep** (`nBones` = 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, read the error knee) remains the principled way to settle it, with two honest caveats: bone count is an **input**, not an output; and the lab has no example sevengill meshes, so the input must be manufactured, making the "optimal" count a property of the animator's assumptions.

**Revised recommended architecture, and it has changed materially since the previous draft.**

1. **Replace the star skeleton with a serial midline chain of ~10–20 segments**, then compress to **~4–6 learned modes** by PCA over tangent angles — the eigenworm/eigenfish construction, ported via **Tierpsy Tracker's MIT-licensed skeleton→eigen-projection pipeline** rather than written from scratch, with SAM2 (already in SharkScarAnnotator) as the segmenter in front of it. Allocate DOF **non-uniformly** — sparse anteriorly, dense posteriorly — per Webb & Keyes' amplitude-envelope result. Budget explicitly for **high-curvature self-occlusion**, which is exactly where the eigenworm basis broke down for coiled worms and where a shark's tail crosses in front of its trunk.
2. **Build the canonical chart as a VMTK-style (s, φ) tube chart, with *s* on the CENTRELINE and φ from a PARALLEL-TRANSPORT frame.** Both choices are load-bearing and both are verified from VMTK's own documentation. *s* on the centreline rather than integrated on the skin is what keeps the coordinate bend-invariant and dumps strain error into the image metric instead — **TubULAR, the closest prior work, integrates *s* along the surface and concedes in its own README that "deformation of the surface in 3D changes the mapping to the plane."** Parallel transport rather than Frenet is what survives curvature inflections, which a swimming shark crosses on **every tailbeat** — VMTK rejects Frenet explicitly, and a completely independent lineage (the reversible colon projection) converged on rotation-minimising frames for the same reason. **For a planar bend, which lateral undulation nearly is, parallel transport accrues zero net rotation, so φ is bend-invariant essentially exactly.** Anchor *s*=0 at a gill slit and **re-anchor periodically at the gill-slit contours** to correct accumulated drift.
3. **Compute the chart ONCE, OFFLINE, ON THE REST-POSE TEMPLATE, and carry it to posed instances through the existing LBS bijection.** This is the design change worth the most, and **it is something no medical or developmental-biology system can do — organs have no rest-pose template, and a shark does.** It eliminates per-frame centreline extraction, which the lab cannot perform anyway from monocular RGB in turbid water, and makes chart stability exact by construction. The residual error is then only SharkSMPL fitting error plus skin strain — **never chart instability**.
4. **Terminate the tube chart at the anatomical boundaries where the tube stops being a tube.** Head forward of the first gill slit is a **cap**, not a tube, and φ degenerates at the pole — treat as a separate disk patch. The caudal peduncle compresses into a keel and the cross-section stops being star-shaped about the medial axis, so the "one ray at angle φ hits the surface once" assumption fails — this is precisely the **obstruction problem** the colon literature names. **Terminate at the precaudal pit / caudal keel — the lab's existing `caudal_notch` joint is the natural terminator — and treat the caudal fin as a separate 2D patch.**
5. **Exclude the fins from the identity surface, and note what that buys.** Conformal maps produce near-exponential area distortion in long thin protrusions (the canonical example in the parameterization literature is literally the Stanford bunny's ears), so any conformal scheme on a fin-bearing shark crushes the fins to ~zero area or blows up the trunk. Three exits exist — VMTK-style branch decomposition with harmonic stretching at insertions, BFF cone singularities with automatic minimal-area-distortion placement, or simple exclusion. **For sevengill re-ID the signal is speckle and scars on the FLANK, so the pragmatic answer is: canonical domain = trunk tube only, fin insertion curves retained as landmarks. This also DISPOSES of the second-dorsal problem rather than patching it — a fin-free trunk chart does not care that *Notorynchus* has one dorsal.**
6. **Choose the distortion trade deliberately, not by default.** Conformal preserves angles and wrecks area; authalic preserves area and wrecks angles. For pattern re-ID the signal is speckle topology and relative position, and DINOv2/MiewID descriptors were trained on natural imagery where local shape matters — **so conformal is the right default, to keep speckle blobs round.** But the moment anyone wants **scar area in cm²** off the chart, or to compare scar sizes across individuals or track healing, conformal is wrong. The mature answer is a **landmark-driven quasiconformal** map (MICCAI 2014) — bounded angle distortion, greatly reduced area distortion, **and landmark constraints that force the gill slits, dorsal insertion, pelvics and cloaca to fixed canonical positions across individuals**, which is what re-ID actually requires. And note **the ranking of flattening methods INVERTS depending on whether you measure local or global, angular or metric distortion** (Ju et al. 2005) — so decide what you care about first.
7. **Design for INVERTIBILITY from day one**, per the reversible-projection line. SharkScarAnnotator stores SAM2 masks in image space; to accumulate scars into the chart and then show a human reviewer where a scar sits on the animal, **the map must invert.** Retrofitting this is expensive.
8. **Do not promise rigid comparison in canonical space.** The honest precedent is prone/supine CT colonography: even *with* a conformal flattening and matched ordered landmarks (96.0/96.1% matching accuracy), they still needed a **nonrigid B-spline registration on the flattened domain** to get correspondence. **Flatten, then nonrigidly register scar maps; pair the chart with deformation-tolerant descriptors, never rigid template matching.**

**And the cheap control the whole plan must beat.** The unfolded-cube result in CT colonography — six 90° projections per centreline station, no conformal machinery at all — achieved **99.5% surface visibility vs 93.8% for a conventional fly-through**, better inter-observer agreement, and faster reading, with **no significant sensitivity/specificity difference.** A cheap per-station multi-view projection captured most of the coverage benefit. **Cost that as the baseline before committing to the chart.**

### 5. What supervision do self-supervised UV methods actually require, and what are their per-individual data requirements?

**"Self-supervised" is doing enormous work in these titles.** Every method in this family replaces manual correspondence labels with *some other* required asset — a template mesh, a foreground mask, a pretrained normal estimator, a pretrained CSE model, calibrated cameras, or a very large image collection. **The full cost table is Q5b above.** Three findings from it matter more than any individual row.

**First, compute and per-individual data requirements are systematically unpublished.** I fetched repo pages and raw READMEs for RAC, lab4d, MagicPony, 3DAnimals, ARTIC3D, Animal Avatars, NeuTex, SMALST, BITE, DOVE, A-CSM, CSM and CMR — **none** states GPU type, GPU count, or training time. Only BANMo, Unicorn and AtlasNet publish real numbers. And beyond BANMo's 10–11 videos, essentially **no released method publishes a per-individual image count, view-coverage requirement, or resolution requirement** — because virtually all of them are *category-level* methods with no notion of an individual at all. **The specific numbers this question asks for largely do not exist in the released documentation.**

**Second, the per-category vs per-individual distinction is the deepest structural objection to the whole research programme.** CMR, UMR, CSM, A-CSM, Unicorn, MagicPony, 3D-Fauna, Ponymation, DOVE, LASSIE, Hi-LASSIE and ARTIC3D all build **one canonical surface for a species.** None models or evaluates individual identity. The only genuinely per-individual methods are BANMo and Animal Avatars — and both **invert the re-ID problem**: to build the per-individual canonical model you must already know which frames belong to that individual. That is circular, and it is independent of species.

**Third — and this replaces the previous draft's weakest paragraph — there IS now published evidence about what pose/surface normalization buys, and it is modest to null.** The draft said "there is no published evidence that identity comparison in a canonical UV space outperforms 2D re-ID, for any species," then leaned on a "5.4%" figure from the seed paper that **the repair pass could not corroborate at all and which is now dropped.** The corrected picture, from the domain where the measurements actually exist:

- **The only genuine internal with/without-3D ablation recovered anywhere: 3DInvarReID (ICCV 2023), Rank-1 28.4 (3D modules discarded) → 36.3 (full model) = +7.9 points (derived).** And that is measured in the **cross-dataset, long-term, cloth-changing regime most favourable to shape invariance**, on humans with an excellent template. **Read it as the optimistic bound.**
- **Stennett et al.'s zebra result — 48.0% → 56.8%, +8.8 points (derived) — is the single published animal measurement, and it must now be downgraded.** The authors call it "a small study," and **MegaDescriptor-L-384 and L-224 both score 1.0000 on SMALST**, the dataset the gain was measured on. A strong generic 2D baseline **saturates it.** So the figures measure the headroom of that paper's particular backbone, **not the intrinsic payoff of pose normalization.** The honest phrasing is: *the single published measurement of an explicit 3D pose-normalization payoff is a ~9-point gain in a self-described small study on a dataset a modern generic baseline already solves — the payoff for sevengills is unmeasured, and this number cannot stand in for it.*
- **Explicit 3D representation buys little even in its own ablation.** OG-Net (TNNLS 2022, MIT, full README table fetched) reaches 88.81 Rank-1 on Market-1501; its improvement from base to best variant is **+1.99 (derived), attributable to depth and Circle loss, not pose normalization.** The paper's own headline claim is **parameter efficiency, not accuracy.**
- **In the best-instrumented sub-literature — faces — the result is null to negative.** Frontalizing training and test images for deep features does **not** significantly improve recognition, and 3D frontalization shows **no significant improvement over simple 2D alignment** (Banerjee et al., arXiv:1610.04823); a 2025 model-agnostic study found **naive head-pose correction DEGRADES accuracy** (arXiv:2512.03199).
- **Margins that look like normalization deltas but are not:** DSA-reID's +10.9/+7.8 on CUHK03 and FD-GAN's +8.2 over SVDNet are both **comparisons against other papers' methods**, not internal ablations. Do not quote them as pose-normalization gains.
- **Every system in this literature quietly concedes the point architecturally.** **PN-GAN** does not use pose-normalized features alone — it generates 8 canonical poses, trains a second model on them, and **fuses** those features with features from the **original** images, calling the two complementary. **FD-GAN** uses pose **only at training time**; at test there is no pose input and no extra compute. **SAN** reconstructs the aligned UV texture with a decoder as **auxiliary supervision** and **discards the decoder at inference.** **CSCL**, having built a bespoke dense-correspondence dataset, still **fuses** surface-embedding shape features with global RGB. **Three independent teams converging on "pose/surface normalization is a regularizer, not a pipeline stage."**

**What this means for the sevengill programme, stated plainly.** *(a)* The measured ceiling on humans — where template, annotations and detectors are vastly better than anything that exists for Hexanchiformes — is roughly **+8 Rank-1 points in the most favourable regime, and approximately zero in the best-controlled face experiments. Do not budget for more than single-digit gains.** *(b)* The cost floor is visible: **CSCL needed 80–125 dense 2D-3D correspondence annotations PER IMAGE** through a custom system; **DensePose-Chimps needed 930 instances / 654 images** at ~3 points each just to add one proximal species; **DensePose itself needed 50 K humans and >5 M correspondences**. The lab has **zero sevengill images, no sevengill template, and no species column in the annotator schema.** *(c)* **SharkSMPL cannot support the test-time-canonicalization version of this hypothesis today regardless**: no UV atlas exists in the model code, the 91 "dense surface landmarks" are `torch.linspace(0, num_verts-1, 91)` one-hot vertex indices — **a canonical surface map needs correspondence, and linspace indices are not correspondence** — and the star skeleton exposes ~2 lateral-bending DOF for an animal whose defining trait is an elongate laterally-flexible trunk. *(d)* **The FD-GAN/SAN pattern — pose or surface reconstruction as an auxiliary decoder head during training, discarded at inference — is the only variant of this hypothesis reachable from the lab's current assets, and it is the variant the human literature actually endorses.** A weak deformation model is far less damaging as a training signal than as a test-time canonicalizer. *(e)* **The texture-over-geometry finding cuts both ways and is the one genuinely encouraging signal**: the sevengill identity signal is speckling and scarring, i.e. **texture**, and the 3D-re-ID literature says texture is where the signal lives — but the same finding says the **geometry/shape branch, the SharkSMPL half, is the weak contributor.**

**One route out of the cost floor that the previous draft missed entirely.** The geometry-processing/medical route requires **no annotation, no training, no GPU and no per-individual data at all** — VMTK, BFF, OptCuts and SLIM consume **a single mesh** and run on CPU in seconds to minutes, under BSD and MIT. **If the chart is computed once on the rest-pose template and carried by LBS (Q4, recommendation 3), the entire dense-correspondence annotation bill — the thing that makes CSE, CSCL and DensePose unaffordable here — disappears.** That is the single largest cost asymmetry in this report, and it is the strongest practical argument for the tube-chart formulation over the learned-correspondence formulation.

**And the missing prior measurement, which should be run before any of it.** Nobody has measured how much viewpoint and body bend actually cost in animal re-ID. **The lab can measure it on its own data this month**: stratify white-shark re-ID accuracy by relative viewing angle and body bend across the 661 GB / 4,850-video corpus, using the existing YOLOv8n-pose output as a bend/aspect proxy. **If viewpoint costs little, pose normalization cannot buy much by construction, and the question is settled before any UV atlas work begins.**

### 6. Do wildlife re-ID benchmarks include pose-normalization baselines to compare against?

**Effectively no — with exactly one partial exception.** I read the official MegaDescriptor protocol documentation verbatim and listed the `wildlife_tools/features/` module directory. The entire pipeline is **bbox crop → resize → global deep descriptor → cosine similarity → 1-NN.** The features package contains only `__init__.py`, `deep.py`, `gluefactory_fix.py`, `local.py`, `memory.py`. There is **no DensePose module, no CSE module, no UV/atlas module, no TPS or keypoint-warping module, no rectification module** anywhere in the reference toolkit covering ~90 public re-ID datasets. The absence is structural, not a documentation oversight. **Nothing in the entire registry ships a canonical 3D surface parameterization for any aquatic species; the nearest 3D entry is SMALST, which is synthetic rendered zebras. The comparison arm has to be built by the lab.**

**The one exception is ATRW**, which distributes 15 pose keypoints **on the re-ID split itself** and whose ICCV'19 CVWC challenge was won in *both* tracks by an explicitly part-pose-aligned method (Apache-2.0). That is the only released, licensed pose-alignment re-ID baseline in the entire animal re-ID space. ⚠️ Its quadruped ear/shoulder/paw/hip schema has zero hexanchiform analogue, so the *process* precedent transfers and the schema does not — and part-cropping into rigid boxes would **fragment the continuous flank speckle field** a sevengill's identity lives in.

**Two things frequently mistaken for pose normalization are not.** **MiewID's** only geometry is a 1-DOF in-plane bbox rotation (`theta`) — straightening the principal axis does nothing for an S-bent trunk, and its README documents no pose-invariance claim at all. **WildFusion**, verified from source, contains **no RANSAC, no homography, no fundamental-matrix estimation, no geometric verification** — LightGlue/LoFTR correspondences are collapsed to a scalar and the geometry discarded. It is **alignment-free matching, a competitor to canonical UV mapping rather than an instance of it**, and it produces no persistent inspectable identity map, which is exactly what a biologist-auditable catalogue needs. ⚠️ **And note the naming trap in the deployed shark stack: Sharkbook's PIE literally stands for "Pose Invariant Embeddings," so "pose-invariant re-ID for sharks" is already a CLAIMED capability in production. The honest distinction is that PIE/MiewID achieve invariance by LEARNING it from data on a locally-rigid patch, while canonical 3D surface mapping would achieve it GEOMETRICALLY over the whole deformable body — that has to be stated and then demonstrated, not assumed.**

**The benchmark is nonetheless, accidentally, the strongest quantitative argument in this report — and the aquatic split is the decision-relevant part.** MegaDescriptor-L-384 averages **79.30% Top-1 across 29 re-ID datasets** *(derived from the repo's per-dataset CSV; L-224 averages 75.09%)*, and performance tracks **medium and rigidity**, not species difficulty. Face and part crops score 91–99% (MacaqueFaces 99.04, CZoo 99.05, SeaTurtleIDHeads 91.18); rigid-marking sets 96–100% (StripeSpotter 98.17; four of the five cattle sets run 96.46–100 — but **FriesianCattle2015 is 55.00**, so "cattle" is not a uniform band and should not be quoted as a range); tigers 94.33. **The aquatic rows collapse: WhaleSharkID 62.02, BelugaID 66.48, NDD20 67.42, ZindiTurtleRecall 74.40, HumpbackWhaleID 77.81, SealID 78.66, NOAARightWhale 40.26, HappyWhale 34.30 — with AAUZebraFish's 99.93 an artifact of a controlled indoor tank with six individuals over one day, not comparable to field imagery. Averaging the ten aquatic rows gives 69.25% against 84.59% for the nineteen terrestrial rows — a roughly 15-point gap (both group means and the gap are derived by us from the per-dataset CSV, not reported in the repo).** **The single most relevant row for this proposal — WhaleSharkID, free-swimming elasmobranchs matched on natural spot pattern in open water — is 62.02%, near the bottom third.** The honest read: **a strong species-agnostic 2D baseline loses substantial accuracy on free-swimming aquatic subjects, so there IS real headroom — but the headroom is not yet demonstrated to be recoverable by pose normalization specifically.**

**The field's standard answer to pose variation is visible in the data, and it is the escape hatch a sevengill does not have.** SeaTurtleIDHeads exists *because* cropping to a rigid, uniquely-patterned part works: 91.18 for head crops versus 25–62 for deformable-body sets. **A sevengill's trunk offers no such part** — which is precisely the gap a canonical surface would fill, and precisely why the two existing sevengill programmes both retreat to the head/gill region instead.

**And the experiment the previous draft could not propose, because it did not know these datasets existed.** **Run it this month, on `wildlife-tools`, with zero new data collection:**

1. **Melops (CC BY 4.0, 24,578 images / 9,861 individuals, PIT-tag ground truth), four conditions: head crop, body crop, headless crop, and cross-orientation (enroll left, query right).** The part ablation is a **one-line constructor argument** (`bbox ∈ {"body","head","headless"}`); the viewpoint protocol is an existing `orientation` column. Evaluate **MegaDescriptor, MiewID zero-shot, and DINOv2.** Use an **open-set / one-shot protocol** — *(derived)* 24,578 / 9,861 ≈ **2.5 images per individual**, so many identities are singletons. **The realistic operating point is the 0.35 one-shot accuracy reported on this same wrasse population (arXiv:2301.00596), not the 0.88 hundred-shot figure.**
2. **Replicate the part ablation on two more clades so the finding cannot be dismissed as a corkwing-wrasse artefact: SeaTurtleIDHeads vs SeaTurtleID2022, and SealID's pattern patches vs whole animals** (both wild, deformable-bodied, multiple poses).
3. **Add the view ablation with FishFaceID** (MIT code; matched Overhead / Underwater / Mixed subsets per species) — **Melops gives the PART axis, FishFaceID gives the VIEW axis, and together they cover both without new collection.**
4. **Use AAUZebraFish's `turning` flag as the only available bend sanity check** — six individuals over one day is not an identification experiment, but it is the only labelled body-deformation state on a free-swimming fish in the entire corpus. Show that a bend-invariant embedding degrades less on `turning==True` frames than a naive baseline.
5. **Read the salmon lateral-line paper before writing more SharkSMPL code** (arXiv:2605.18038): identity anchored to a predicted body-axis curve, patches sampled in that frame, **mAP 0.609 → 0.860 cross-camera versus a full-image baseline.** It is a **1-D body-axis normalisation, not a canonical 3D surface**, on an elongate free-swimming laterally-bending fish — and it suggests most of the available gain may be reachable at a fraction of the cost of a 16-joint LBS model. The cattle work points the same way: **spine orientation as a metadata channel raised rank-1 from 0.822 to 0.894**, with no articulated 3D model at all.

**How to read the outcome.** **If headless ≈ body, identity is distributed across the deformable trunk and pose-normalisation of that trunk is worth building. If head ≫ headless, the whole canonical-surface programme is solving a problem the biology does not have**, and the lab should instead invest in a rigid head/gill-region matcher — which is exactly what the existing sevengill Wildbook already does, and what a decade of La Jolla matches suggests works. **That is a cheap, decisive, in-hand experiment on the central question, and it requires no 3D machinery, no template, no annotation and no sevengill data.**

⚠️ **Two things this experiment does NOT settle, stated so nobody over-reads it.** *(1)* Melops fish are handled, shot against a standardised white board with a colour reference card, and are **not laterally bending in frame**; its `orientation` is a two-valued side flip, not a body-axis bend parameter. **The canonical-3D-surface leg of the hypothesis has NO existing dataset that can validate it — across every fish and elasmobranch re-ID dataset enumerated here, not one carries per-image body-bend, articulated pose, or a canonical surface parameterisation.** *(2)* Nothing in this corpus removes the schema problem: **no public dataset uses seven gill slits or a single posterior dorsal fin**, so `config/keypoints_16.yaml` id 3 ("Back edge of last (5th) gill slit") and ids 10–11 (`second_dorsal_*`) remain undefined for *Notorynchus* whichever dataset is used. **Melops' 16 anatomically-defined keypoints are a TEMPLATE for how to author a schema, not a substitute for authoring one.**
