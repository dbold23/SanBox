## (c) Three candidate approaches to prototype

**Read these four facts before reading the approaches. All three are scoped on them.**

**1. You have no sevengill data, and no place to put it.** Nothing in your four repos contains a single labelled *Notorynchus cepedianus* image. The 661 GB / 4,850-video archive is white shark. The 689/163 keypoint split is white shark, Schema v2. The one template mesh is a lamniform great white. And `SharkScarAnnotator` has **no species column anywhere in its schema** — it implicitly assumes *C. carcharias*, so there is literally no field in which a sevengill record could be stored today. Data collection **plus a schema migration** is a prerequisite to every approach below, not a footnote to one of them. It is written out as P0 below and repeated inside each approach because it is the thing most likely to be skipped.

**2. Sevengill photo-ID already works, in the field, on the *anterior* body.** This corrects the previous draft, which rested the entire premise on one unverifiable 2016 abstract (Housiaux, "n=23" — **strike that number, it could not be corroborated in any form** [UNVERIFIED]). The real evidence base is at least three independent groups: Lewis, Dawson & Rayment (*J. Fish Biol.* 97(4):987–995, 2020, doi:10.1111/jfb.14453) identified **149 individuals across 357 encounters from 133 baited-underwater-video deployments** at Stewart Island and fitted a full capture–recapture model on them, keying on dorsal spot "constellations" [SEARCH]; Ocean Sanctuaries' Sevengill Shark ID Project has run in La Jolla since 2010 on Wildbook, matching the **freckling between the nares and the gill slits — the lateral head patch** — and reports ~76 matched individuals over 2010–2020 [SEARCH, advocacy/press sources; treat as order-of-magnitude, not a number to publish]; and Save Our Seas funded a dedicated sevengill photo-ID effort at Miller's Point, False Bay [SEARCH]. The incumbent method is a **small, locally-rigid anterior patch matched with off-the-shelf 2D matchers** (HotSpotter, PIE, MiewID). That is your baseline, and it is not a strawman.

**3. Pose normalization buys very little, even where it has every advantage.** This is the single most decision-relevant finding of the repair pass and it must shape the sequencing, not sit in a footnote. In *human* re-ID — where a mature SMPL template, DensePose-scale annotation, and near-perfect detectors all exist — the only genuine internal with/without-3D ablation recovered is 3DInvarReID (ICCV 2023): Rank-1 **28.4 → 36.3**, i.e. **+7.9 points (derived by subtraction)** [SEARCH, single source], and that is measured in the cross-dataset cloth-changing regime *most* favourable to shape invariance. OG-Net (TNNLS 2022, MIT) shows explicit 3D representation does **not** beat strong 2D re-ID at all — its Market-1501 gain of **+1.99 Rank-1 (86.82 → 88.81, derived)** comes from network depth and Circle loss, not from the 3D [PRIMARY, repo README table]; the paper's own claim is parameter efficiency. And in *faces* — the best-instrumented, most rigid, most heavily templated object in vision — the result is null-to-negative: 3D frontalization shows **no significant improvement over simple 2D alignment** (Banerjee et al., arXiv:1610.04823) [SEARCH], and a 2025 replication finds naive head-pose correction **degrades** recognition (arXiv:2512.03199) [SEARCH, single source]. Every system in that literature quietly concedes the point architecturally: PN-GAN *fuses* pose-normalized features with original-image features and calls them complementary; FD-GAN uses pose **only at training time**; SAN reconstructs a canonical UV texture as an auxiliary decoder target and **discards the decoder at inference** [PRIMARY, README]. Three independent teams converged on "canonicalization is a regularizer, not a pipeline stage." **Budget single-digit points, not a step change.**

**4. The one published measurement of an explicit 3D pose-normalization payoff in an animal is ~9 points, in a self-described small study, on a dataset a generic 2D baseline already saturates.** Stennett, Rubenstein & Burghardt (arXiv:2206.02261, VAIB 2022) fit 3D models to Grevy's zebra and matched back-projected texture: **48.0% → 56.8%, an 8.8-point gain (derived)** [SEARCH]. But MegaDescriptor-L-384 and L-224 both score **1.0000 on SMALST** — the dataset that gain was measured on — verified first-hand from the wildlife-tools baseline CSV [PRIMARY]. So those figures measure the headroom of that paper's backbone, not the intrinsic payoff of pose normalization. Do not let anyone quote 48.0 → 56.8 as evidence that 3D wins.

## Approach 1 — Rigid-part vs deformable-body re-ID, tested on Melops first, then ported to sevengills

*The cheap route, and — this is the change from the previous draft — **most of it can be run to a decision in weeks, on free data, before you collect a single sevengill image**.*

### What it is

Two phases. **Phase 1A is the decisive experiment and it needs no sevengill data at all.**

**Phase 1A — the Melops ablation.** The `Melops` dataset (corkwing wrasse, *Symphodus melops*) is a wild fish re-ID corpus of **24,578 images / 9,861 individuals over a 7-year span (Norway, 2018–2024)**, with identity ground truth from **PIT tags** — hardware, not human photo-matching consensus — under **CC BY 4.0**, on Zenodo record 17404087, wrapped in `wildlife-datasets` [all PRIMARY, verified from `melops.py`]. The reason it is the headline item: it ships **`bbox_body`, `bbox_head` and `bbox_headless` as three separate columns**, with a constructor parameter `bbox ∈ {"body","head","headless"}` [PRIMARY]. It also carries an **`orientation` column** (assigned literally `df["orientation"] = df["side"]`) [PRIMARY], giving a cross-viewpoint protocol off the shelf.

That means the entire rigid-part-vs-deformable-body question — *"does fish identity live in a small rigid anterior patch, or in the whole deformable body?"*, which is the question the whole sevengill programme turns on — is a **one-argument change**, not a data-engineering project. Run MegaDescriptor, MiewID and DINOv2 zero-shot and fine-tuned across four conditions: **head crop / body crop / headless crop / cross-orientation (enroll left, query right)**, using `wildlife-tools`. Replicate on a second clade for free with **SeaTurtleIDHeads vs SeaTurtleID2022** (the same head-vs-whole-image pairing, WACV 2024) and **SealID**, which ships small pattern *patches* alongside whole-animal images and whose authors state the patches suffice for identification (CC BY 4.0) [PRIMARY]. Three clades agreeing is very hard to dismiss as a corkwing artefact.

**Phase 1B — sevengill 2D re-ID with midline rectification.** Only after 1A. Detect and segment (YOLOv8n-pose retrained on the P0-a schema + SAM2), fit a spline midline through the keypoint chain, resample the flank *normal to that curve* into a fixed-size rectangle, match with MegaDescriptor / MiewID / WildFusion. The rectification is a 1-D analogue of unwrapping that absorbs lateral bending with no 3D at all, and it has direct precedent: the salmon patch-ensemble work predicts the **lateral line** and extracts texture-anchored patches in that body-anchored frame, reporting **cross-camera mAP 0.609 → 0.860** versus a full-image baseline (arXiv:2605.18038; figures are SEARCH-snippet, confirm against the PDF) [PRIMARY for the repo, SEARCH for the numbers]. A 1-D body-axis parameterisation may capture most of the available gain at a fraction of the cost of any 3D model — which matters given your `core/skeleton.py` currently offers ~2 lateral-bending DOF anyway.

### Concrete data requirement

**Phase 1A: zero new data.** Download Melops from Zenodo 17404087 from a machine with open egress (the `wildlife-datasets` wrapper pulls the body-crop archive plus metadata by default; head and headless archives must be requested explicitly). SeaTurtleID/Heads and SealID likewise. Compute: fine-tuning runs on one GPU. **Realistic elapsed time: 2–4 weeks including the write-up.**

One protocol constraint, **derived, not from any paper**: 24,578 / 9,861 ≈ **2.5 images per individual**, so many identities are singletons or pairs. This forces an **open-set / one-shot protocol**, not a closed-set classification split. Calibrate expectations against the prior method paper on the same population (arXiv:2301.00596, NLDL 2023): **one-shot 0.35, 5-shot 0.56, 100-shot 0.88** [SEARCH, snippet — confirm before quoting]. One-shot 0.35 is the realistic operating point.

**Phase 1B, sevengill:**
- **Individuals: 40–60 minimum.** Anchors: Lewis 2020 reached 149 from 133 BUV deployments; Ocean Sanctuaries reached ~76 over a decade of diver contributions; WhaleSharkID has 543. Below ~40 you cannot separate "the method failed" from "the gallery was too small."
- **Images per individual: 8–15, hard floor 3.** WhaleSharkID averages ~14; SeaTurtleIDHeads ~19. Melops at ~2.5 is the warning case.
- **View coverage: near-lateral, both flanks, tracked as separate identities.** Wildbook maintains separate left/right whale-shark spot sets; Melops tracks `side` as a first-class column; the Grevy's zebra pipeline states each side needs separate classification. Budget 2× your target catalogue and **do not mirror-augment**.
- **Resolution:** flank ≥256 px on the long axis after crop (MegaDescriptor-L-384 runs at 518², MiewID at 440²).
- **Annotation:** ~2 min/frame for keypoints + side + ID at 60 individuals × 12 images × 2 sides ≈ 1,440 frames ≈ **48 hours**, plus roughly the same again for identity adjudication → call it **~100 annotator-hours**, on top of 4–8 weeks of field/archive sourcing. SAM2 removes the mask cost.
- **Copy Melops' field protocol for free:** it ships a `keypoints_white_card` column and a colour-extraction correction pipeline, i.e. **an in-frame colour reference card** [PRIMARY]. In turbid green kelp water this is the cheapest available fix for the contrast problem, and it costs a piece of laminated card.

### Why it might fail on sevengills

**(1) The identity signal may not be recoverable at your contrast.** Dark-on-light speckling over a grey-brown dorsum is lower spatial-frequency and lower contrast than seal pelage or leopard rosettes — both photographed *in air*. La Jolla is diver-held camera in typically **10–20 ft (3–6 m) visibility**, best days 30–40 ft, in kelp, with the aggregation concentrated March–April [SECONDARY, dive-operator sources — indicative, not measurements; get real figures from SCCOOS if the number matters]. The generic-baseline evidence points the same way: on the wildlife-tools sweep, MegaDescriptor-L-384 averages **79.30% across 29 re-ID datasets** but the ten aquatic rows average **69.25%** against **84.59%** for the nineteen terrestrial rows — **a ~15-point gap (all three figures derived by us from the per-dataset CSV, not reported in the repo)** [PRIMARY for the per-dataset values]. The closest analogue to your target, **WhaleSharkID, scores 62.02%**.

**(2) The pattern may not be stable** — see P0-d. And pigmentation instability is documented *in a shark*: melanism in a white shark produced a **33% reduction in an islet's size over 9 months**, the newly melanised region 10% darker and not matching the original pattern (Robbins & Fox, *Mar. Freshw. Res.* mf12208) [SEARCH; author/year are the least-corroborated details in the scan].

**(3) Scars are a decaying, not a durable, channel in elasmobranchs.** Do not repeat the previous draft's flat "scars are unproven" — the correct claim is mechanistic and species-specific. Scar-based ID is *validated at scale* where injuries permanently deform tissue: USGS/FWC's manatee MIPS has run on thousands of individuals since 1967 and underpins peer-reviewed federal survival estimates (Langtimm et al., *Mar. Mamm. Sci.* 20(3):438–463, 2004) [SEARCH]. Sharks are the adverse case: in blacktip reef sharks a ~20 cm deep bite wound closed almost completely **within 3 days** and fully recovered within 40; mating scars healed within a month; a wound and scar at an incision point were **undetectable within 179 days** (*Conserv. Physiol.* 3(1):cov062) [SEARCH]. **Derived, label as derived:** a sevengill scar signature plausibly has a half-life of months to a few years, so a scar-only gallery would need re-enrolment on a months-to-years cadence. Reef manta lacerations healed to **5% of initial wound length within 295 days** but the residue then stabilised into a distinctive pattern still present **>3 years later**, with **243 of 1,071 individuals (22.7%) scarred** [SEARCH] — so keep scars as a **time-weighted corroborating channel**, not the primary key.

**(4) Your detector will not transfer.** YOLOv8n-pose at Pose mAP50 0.578 is white-shark-trained on a schema 12.5% of which is undefined for hexanchiforms. Sevengill keypoints are annotated from scratch.

### Kill criterion

> ⚠️ **What the Melops ablation does and does not settle.** Melops fish are *handled* — shot against a standardised white board with a colour reference card — and are **not laterally bending in frame**; its `orientation` field is a two-valued side flip, not a body-axis bend parameter. So this experiment settles **where identity lives** (a rigid part vs the deformable flank), which is the question that decides whether the canonical-surface programme is worth starting. It **cannot** measure what pose normalization buys on a bending body, because nothing in the frame bends. **No existing fish or elasmobranch dataset can validate the canonical-3D leg** — that requires sevengill data the lab does not yet have (P0-c). Do not let a clean 1A result be read as evidence that unwrapping works.

**Phase 1A kill/redirect (this is the important one, and it fires in weeks):** if **head-crop Rank-1 exceeds headless-crop Rank-1 by ≥15 points** on Melops under the one-shot open-set protocol, the identity signal in a fish is concentrated in the rigid anterior part, the whole canonical-3D-surface programme is solving a problem the biology does not have, and the lab should redirect to a rigid head/gill-region matcher — which is also exactly what the existing sevengill Wildbook already does. Conversely if **headless ≈ body (within 5 points)**, identity is genuinely distributed over the deformable trunk and Approach 2 earns a hearing. Note the ecological prior points at the first outcome: a decade of successful sevengill matching in La Jolla uses the nares-to-gill freckle patch, the least-bending region of the animal.

**Phase 1B kill:** **Rank-1 below 50% on a time-separated split (query sightings ≥6 months after gallery) at 40+ individuals.** Time-separated, not random — CzechLynx ships exactly this protocol (`split-time_open`) because random splits massively overstate re-ID on drifting marks. External anchors: MegaDescriptor's 62.02% on WhaleSharkID (a spot-patterned elasmobranch on a *less* deformable body — a ceiling, not a target); **90.3% with classic I3S over up to 496 days** on blue-spotted ribbontail ray, validated by physical mark-recapture (*Sci. Rep.* 2024, doi:10.1038/s41598-024-68302-0) [SEARCH]; and the honest ground-truth ceiling, **~85% concordance between fin photo-ID and microsatellite genotypes over 5 years** in white sharks (Gubili et al. 2009) [SEARCH]. Separately: **if midline rectification does not beat the raw-crop baseline by ≥3 Rank-1 points in the same experiment, the rectification idea is dead** and you learned it cheaply.

Adoption bar to quote to the PI: Moskvyak et al. state biologists require **top-10 ≥ 95%** for a re-ID system to be adopted (arXiv:1902.10847) [SEARCH].

## Approach 2 — Centerline tube flattening: an (arc-length × circumferential-angle) chart, fins as separate charts

*The genuine canonical-3D-surface attempt — redesigned. **Do not port a tetrapod DensePose/CSE pipeline wholesale.** Medicine has shipped exactly this operation on bent tubes for 25 years, in production, with permissive code.*

### What it is

The previous draft proposed retopologising a great-white mesh and training a Continuous Surface Embeddings head on it. That route is now the *fallback*, for three reasons established in the gap-fill. First, the complete DensePose CSE mesh registry is **thirteen meshes — one human plus twelve placental mammals** (`smpl_27554, chimp_5029, cat_5001, cat_7466, sheep_5004, zebra_5002, horse_5004, giraffe_5002, elephant_5002, dog_5002, dog_7466, cow_5002, bear_4936`), with **zero fish, zero non-tetrapods** [PRIMARY, fetched `builtin.py`]; the animal models are obtained by finetuning *human* models and the method is literally called "Transferring Dense Pose to **Proximal** Animal Classes." A hexanchiform has no proximal donor. Second, A-CSM's shipped articulation is a handful of rigid parts on a body root — `horse.txt` is 7 parts, `bird.txt` is **3** [PRIMARY, fetched] — which is the *same* star-topology failure as your own skeleton, so it cannot cure it. Third, the annotation currency is real: CSCL, the human execution of exactly this idea, needed a purpose-built dataset with **80–125 dense 2D–3D correspondence annotations per image** [SEARCH].

**The redesign.** Treat the sevengill trunk as what it is: a genus-0 tube. Give it the coordinate system that vascular and colon imaging have used since 2000 — **arc-length `s` along the medial axis × circumferential angle `φ`** — and handle everything that is not a tube as a separate chart.

Six design decisions, each sourced:

1. **Compute `s` on the CENTERLINE and push it to the surface — never integrate it along the skin.** VMTK does exactly this: each vascular segment "is topologically equivalent to a cylinder and can consequently be mapped onto a rectangular parametric space," carrying `AbscissaMetric` (from the curvilinear abscissa on the centerline) and `AngularMetric` (periodic, spanning (−π, +π)) [PRIMARY, fetched VMTK docs]. Contrast **TubULAR** (Nature Methods 2023, MIT, README verified [PRIMARY]), the closest prior work on a *deforming* biological tube, which integrates `s` **along the surface** and therefore concedes in its own README that it is only "surface Lagrangian" because "deformation of the surface in 3D changes the mapping to the plane." Building `s` on the centerline fixes precisely the thing TubULAR concedes. The mechanical justification is the neutral-axis argument: the medial axis of a bent beam is inextensional to first order.
2. **Use parallel transport, not a Frenet frame, to define `φ`.** VMTK explicitly rejects Frenet: "given a starting reference system… the next reference system is generated by moving along the centerline of a small amount and rotating the system on the osculating plane… no artificial torsion is introduced" [PRIMARY]. This matters enormously for a shark: a Frenet normal flips sign at a curvature inflection, and a swimming shark crosses inflections **every tailbeat**, so a Frenet-anchored `φ=0` would be discontinuous mid-stroke. Parallel transport does not flip, and for a *planar* bend — which lateral undulation nearly is — it accrues **zero net rotation**, making `φ` essentially bend-invariant. An independent lineage converged on the same fix: the reversible colon-unfolding work maps vertices "to the rotation-minimizing frames along the centerline… preserves the resolution and topology… and avoids the obstruction problem" [SEARCH]. Two unrelated groups agreeing is the strongest signal available given neither primary source was fetchable.
3. **Anchor `s=0` at anatomy and re-anchor periodically.** VMTK's abscissa origin is settable: "the point with abscissa 0… can be chosen to represent a landmark along a centerline" [PRIMARY]. Your seven gill slits are seven roughly-circumferential landmark curves — ideal iso-`s` contours to pin the origin and correct parallel-transport drift, which is path-dependent and accumulates, so head-end centerline error otherwise propagates as a global `φ` offset down the whole animal.
4. **Compute the chart ONCE, OFFLINE, on the rest-pose template, and carry it to posed instances through the existing LBS bijection.** This is the design change worth the most, and **medicine cannot do it** — organs have no rest-pose template, so every colon needs per-scan centerline extraction from a segmented volume. You have a template. Fit SharkSMPL to the image, then read `(s, φ)` off the template through skinning. This eliminates per-frame centerline extraction entirely — which you could not do anyway from monocular RGB in turbid kelp water — and makes chart stability **exact by construction**. Residual error is then only fit error plus skin strain, never chart instability.
5. **Fins are separate charts; the trunk chart is fin-free.** Conformal maps produce near-exponential area distortion in long thin protrusions (the canonical example in the parameterization literature is the Stanford bunny's ears) [SEARCH]. Three borrowed exits: VMTK-style branch decomposition with harmonic stretching at insertion regions — "vmtkbranchmapping maps and stretches the longitudinal metric to correctly account for the presence of insertion regions at bifurcations" [PRIMARY]; **cone singularities** at fin tips/insertions via Boundary First Flattening, which supports automatic cone placement for minimal area distortion [PRIMARY, README]; or simply excluding fins from the identity surface. For sevengill re-ID the signal is flank speckle and scars, so: **canonical domain = trunk tube only, fin insertion curves retained as landmarks.** This also *dissolves* the `second_dorsal_*` problem rather than patching it — a fin-free trunk chart does not care that *Notorynchus* has one dorsal.
6. **Terminate the tube at both ends and seam it ventrally.** The head is a blunt cap where `φ` degenerates at the pole — terminate the chart at the first gill slit and treat the head as a disk patch. The caudal peduncle compresses into a keel and the cross-section stops being star-shaped about the axis, so a ray cast at angle `φ` no longer hits the surface exactly once (the "obstruction problem") — terminate at the precaudal pit / caudal keel and treat the caudal fin as a separate 2D patch. Your existing `caudal_notch` joint is the natural terminator. The longitudinal seam goes on the **ventral midline**, where the identity signal is weakest. And note for descriptor design: `φ` is **periodic**, and a CNN/ViT on a flat rectangle does not know the left and right edges are adjacent — use circular padding in `φ` (arXiv:2605.16305 raises exactly this seam problem for tube parameterization; preprint, no code, watch-not-depend) [SEARCH].

**The one genuine theoretical guarantee, and it is free to state:** a genus-0 tube with two boundary circles is an annulus, and an annulus has exactly **one** conformal invariant, its modulus [SEARCH]. So the conformal chart to a rectangle is determined by the surface up to two gauge freedoms — a `φ` rotation and an `s` translation — and **both are killed by anatomy** (snout, dorsal midline, gill slits). Combined with Gu & Yau's uniqueness result for genus-0 conformal parameterization (SGP 2003) [SEARCH], this is the cleanest available statement that "canonical" means something well-posed here. Put it in the proposal.

- **P0-b first: the DigitalLife3D licence.** Everything below is modification of that mesh. **UNKNOWN terms = no Approach 2.**

Budget **2–4 weeks of artist time**. Check MorphoSource/oVert first (`Imageomics/pyMorphoSource`, MIT, filters on `taxonomy_gbif` + `media_type="Mesh"`, answers this in an afternoon), but note CT resolves calcified cartilage, not skin, and chondrichthyan endoskeleton scans poorly.
- **A serial spine.** The star skeleton must go. `core/skeleton.py` parents nearly everything to `body_midpoint_dorsal`; only `gill_slit` (head yaw) and `caudal_notch` (tail yaw) express body-axis bending — **~2 lateral-bending DOF for the entire animal**. A medial axis cannot be represented by that. Build a serial chain of **10–20 spine joints**, then compress to **4–6 learned modes**. Justification: four eigenworm modes capture **>95%** of *C. elegans* posture variance (Stephens et al., *PLoS Comput. Biol.* 4:e1000028, 2008; independently restated by Broekmans et al., *eLife* 5:e17227, 2016) [SEARCH, two independent sources], and the fish analogue holds — **three PCs explain >90%** of teleost-larval tail-shape variance, PC1 = turning, PC2/PC3 = oscillation [SEARCH, eLife reviewed preprint 98347]. ### Why it might fail on sevengills

**(1) Skin stretch on the convex flank. This is the real breaker, and it is not fixable — only tolerable.** Bending is **not** an isometry of the skin. The previous draft's claim that "lateral undulation is approximately isometric" and that the trunk is a "developable cylinder" is **wrong and must be deleted**: the fish literature states the opposite explicitly — during lateral bending there is a mediolateral strain gradient with "the greatest shortening occurring near the skin and the least shortening near the backbone" (*Sci. Rep.* 11, s41598-021-88828-x, 2021) [SEARCH]. **The skin is the maximum-strain surface.** Measured, in a shark: sonomicrometry of leopard-shark red muscle at only ~1.0 BL/s gives strain amplitudes of **±3.9% anterior, ±6.6% mid-body, ±4.8% posterior**, in phase with local midline curvature and reproduced at all three sites by a bending-beam model (Donley & Shadwick, *J. Exp. Biol.* 206(7):1117–, 2003) [SEARCH]. Shark red muscle is superficial, so **derived**: skin strain is ≥ those values, and at a hard turn (half-width ~0.10 m, radius of curvature ~0.8–1.0 m) the outer-fibre estimate `r/R` is ~10–12% per flank, i.e. a **~20–25% longitudinal scale differential between the two flanks at the same `s` — antisymmetric, both happening in the same frame**. Also derived: 5% over a ~1.5 m trunk is **~7.5 cm** of flank arc-length change, many scar-diameters.

Biology makes it worse. Shark skin is a pressurised crossed-helical collagen exotendon; fibre angles measured at **45–65°**, consistent with a conserved 40–60° range, and the skin is **more extensible longitudinally (antero-posterior) than dorsoventrally** — most extensible in exactly the direction `s` runs (Hagood et al., *Integr. Comp. Biol.* 63(6):1154, 2023) [SEARCH]. And its stiffness is under **active hydrostatic control varying with swimming speed** (Wainwright, Vosburgh & Hebrank, *Science* 202:747–749, 1978) [SEARCH] — so the same shark at the same bend angle deforms differently at cruise versus burst, meaning **a learned deformation correction cannot be a pure function of pose**. *(Two honesty notes: I could not retrieve any subdermal pressure magnitude — do not quote a kPa figure [UNVERIFIED]; and the exotendon claim was contested in print, with Hebrank & Hebrank finding no external-tendon role in two teleosts, so do not cite it as settled.)*

**Consequence — and it is a design instruction, not a caveat:** build `s` on the centerline so the *coordinate* survives, accept that the *induced metric* does not, and **flatten then nonrigidly register — never rigidly compare canonical charts.** The honest precedent is prone/supine CT colonography: even with a conformal flattening *and* matched ordered landmarks (haustral folds, **96.0% / 96.1%** matching accuracy with and without local collapse), they still needed a landmark-driven nonrigid warp plus an intensity-based **B-spline registration on the flattened domain** to get correspondence (*Med. Image Anal.*, PMID 23845949) [SEARCH]. Expect the same. That ~96% is also the right ceiling to quote: it is what a mature, volumetric, two-scan, same-day, same-individual system achieves. A monocular turbid-water system should not promise better, and saying so is a credibility asset.

**(2) Which distortion do you want? The answer is not universal, and you must choose deliberately.** Conformal preserves angles and wrecks area; authalic preserves area and wrecks angles. The colon literature says plainly that angle-preserving-only maps carry large area distortion, making them less useful for polyp visualization [SEARCH], and the area-preserving branch exists precisely for that (Zhu, Haker & Tannenbaum, MICCAI 2003, and the 2019 OMT supine-to-prone colon registration) [SEARCH]. Meanwhile FreeSurfer optimises **metric** distortion rather than angles — the opposite objective from the colon conformal line. Ju et al. (*NeuroImage* 28(4):869–880, 2005) is the closest thing to a real head-to-head: FreeSurfer wins on *global* metric distortion, LSCM wins on *angular* distortion and on a *local* metric measure — **the ranking inverts depending on which distortion you measure and at what scale** [SEARCH, qualitative ranking only; I quote no numbers]. For pattern re-ID the signal is speckle topology and relative position, and DINOv2/MiewID descriptors were trained on natural imagery where local shape matters, so **conformal is the right default** (keeps speckle blobs round). But the moment anyone wants scar **area in cm²** off the chart, or wound-healing comparisons across time, conformal is wrong. The mature target is a **landmark-driven quasiconformal** map (MICCAI 2014) [SEARCH]: bounded angle distortion, greatly reduced area distortion, **and landmark constraints** — which is the mechanism for forcing the seven gill slits, the dorsal insertion, the pelvics and the cloaca to fixed canonical positions. That is also the honest reframing of your landmark problem: you need a handful of *real* anatomical constraints, not 91 `linspace` vertex indices.

**(3) Ovalization.** Under bending, a tube's cross-section flattens toward an ellipse (Brazier effect) [SEARCH, standard shell-theory result; original attribution not verified]. So a uniform `φ` grid is **not** a uniform surface sampling, and the non-uniformity is pose-dependent. For a shark this is qualitative, not quantitative — the section is already a laterally compressed ellipse and a muscular trunk is not a passive elastic shell — so treat it as a mechanism to test empirically on the template, and present the engineering analogy *as* an analogy.

**(4) Invertibility is a hard requirement nobody has flagged.** The colon literature has an explicit "reversible projection" line [SEARCH]. `SharkScarAnnotator` stores SAM2 masks in *image* space; to accumulate scars into the chart and then show a human reviewer where a scar sits on the actual animal, **the map must invert.** Design for it from day one rather than retrofitting.

**(5) The payoff may not justify any of this.** See fact 3 in the preamble. If canonicalization is worth ~+8 Rank-1 on humans in its best case and ~0 on faces, the prior on a large win here should be low.

### Kill criterion

**Primary, and it is cheap and early:** after the P0-b licence clears and the template exists, **if a VMTK maximal-inscribed-sphere centerline on the sevengill template does not track the vertebral axis to within 5% of body length along ≥90% of the trunk** — i.e. it wanders into fins or the caudal lobe — the chart is not well-defined and the approach stops before any learning. One afternoon in SlicerVMTK.

**Secondary:** **median chart-position error above 10% of body length**, measured by projecting hand-annotated anatomical landmarks (gill slits 1 and 7, pectoral insertion, dorsal insertion, pelvic origin, cloaca, precaudal pit) from ≥50 held-out sevengill frames into `(s, φ)` and comparing against their canonical template positions. Calibration: released animal CSE models reach geodesic error 11.0–13.2 on quadrupeds *with* cycle losses, and CT colonography reaches 96% ordered-landmark matching with volumetric two-scan data.

**Tertiary, the one that actually decides funding:** **if pattern matching read out of the flattened chart fails to beat the Approach 1B midline-rectified 2D baseline by ≥5 Rank-1 points on the same time-separated split, the geometry is not paying for itself.** Given the human evidence (+7.9 best case, +1.99 for explicit 3D on Market, ~0 on faces), **assume this is the likely outcome and design the experiment so that a null result is publishable.**

## Approach 3 — Learn the canonical space instead of authoring it, and consider using it only at training time

*The template-free route, plus the cheaper sibling the human literature actually endorses. Both dissolve the "we have no sevengill template" blocker; one of them dissolves most of the cost too.*

### What it is

**3A — self-supervised pattern unwrapping (this is the baseline Approach 2 must beat, and it is dated 2025, so it postdates your design).** "Unsupervised Pelage Pattern Unwrapping for Animal Re-identification" (arXiv:2506.15369) estimates a geometry-aware UV mapping that unwraps pelage into a **canonical texture space** for re-ID, guided by surface-normal estimation, **self-supervised, with no ground-truth UV annotation**, evaluated on Saimaa ringed seals and leopards — both chosen for distinctive but **highly deformable** patterns — and reports improved accuracy across diverse poses and viewing angles [SEARCH; author list unverified, arxiv egress-blocked, no code located]. It needs **images only**: no template, no 3D, no manual UV. Given that the lab has **zero sevengill images and no species column**, and no sevengill mesh, that is by a wide margin the cheapest data requirement of anything in this document. **The burden this places on Approach 2 is specific and should be stated in the proposal rather than discovered by a reviewer: show that an explicit 3D template plus a centerline tube chart beats a self-supervised image-space UV estimator that needs neither.**

**3B — the auxiliary-decoder variant, which is the version the evidence actually supports.** Do not canonicalize at test time. Train the re-ID encoder with a **surface/UV reconstruction decoder as an auxiliary head, and throw the decoder away at inference.** SAN (Semantics Aligning Network, AAAI 2020) does exactly this — an encoder produces the re-ID feature while a decoder reconstructs the densely aligned full UV texture, trained jointly, decoder **discarded at inference for efficiency** [PRIMARY, fetched README]. FD-GAN (NeurIPS 2018) uses pose **only at training time**, with no auxiliary pose input and no extra compute at test [SEARCH]. **This is the only variant of the canonical-surface hypothesis reachable from your current assets**, because a weak deformation model (your ~2-DOF star skeleton, your 91 `linspace` landmarks) is far less damaging as a *training signal* than as a *test-time canonicalizer*. It also means you do not need a production-grade sevengill UV atlas — only one good enough to be a decoder target.

**3C — template-free canonical shape from video, as the ambitious long tail.** `lab4d-org/lab4d` (**MIT**, © 2023 Gengshan Yang) learns a canonical neural implicit shape plus skeleton and neural blend skinning across casual monocular videos, and — verified from `lab4d/config.py` — exposes `--feature_type ∈ {dinov2, cse}`, so **the CSE dependency that kills this family for sharks is optional**. `--fg_motion=bob` gives a treeless bag-of-bones with a freely chosen bone count. `MagicPony`/`3DAnimals` (MIT) learn a DMTet articulated template from image collections with masks and DINO features only, and MagicPony's *bird* config sets `num_legs: 0`, reducing to a pure 8-bone axial chain — genuinely the right first-order shark skeleton, and a one-value config edit.

### Concrete data requirement

- **3A:** images with masks. That is all. You have SAM2. **The gating question is not data volume but signal:** run a one-day test of monocular surface-normal estimation on real sevengill frames in turbid water. If normals are garbage at 3–6 m visibility, 3A's guidance signal is garbage.
- **3B:** the Approach 1B sevengill catalogue (40–60 individuals, both flanks) **plus** a rough sevengill template good enough to render UV targets. Your Blender domain-randomisation pipeline generates those targets for free — this is the same asset that makes PRNet-style UV position-map regression credible (`yfeng95/PRNet`: **code MIT, but the trained model file is explicitly NOT covered by that grant** [PRIMARY, README + LICENSE]). PRNet needed 3DMM fits over 300W-LP to manufacture its targets; you can render yours.
- **3C:** this is where it hurts. BANMo's released demos are **11 videos for one cat, 10 for one human** — roughly **10 casual monocular videos per individual**, plus per-frame masks, optical flow and dense features, at **4 h on 2× V100 short schedule / 12 h full**, i.e. **8–24 GPU-hours per individual**. MagicPony is 140k iterations at 256², batch 10, for a horse; pan-category 3D-Fauna is **900k iterations at batch 6 on a single GPU** — days to weeks, and it produces one *species* template, not identities. Total-Recon requires **RGB-D**, which is impossible underwater at range — rule it out. Realistic scoping: 30–50 individuals × 5–10 usable clips, **500–1,500 GPU-hours plus a year of collection**.

### Why it might fail on sevengills

**(1) 3C is circular.** BANMo needs many videos *of the same identified individual* to build that individual's model; to know which clips show the same animal you need re-ID; to get re-ID you need the model. The only exit is bootstrapping identities from Approach 1 first. This objection is structural and species-independent.

**(2) Every canonical-shape-from-video method in the corpus is quadruped- or biped-locked in practice.** 3D-Fauna hardcodes `num_legs: 4, num_leg_bones: 3` — its "100+ species" are 100+ quadrupeds. Ponymation ships horse/cow/giraffe/zebra only. Unicorn's categories are birds, cars, rigid ShapeNet classes and horses — nothing elongate, nothing serially articulated, nothing aquatic. And `lab4d/utils/skel_utils.py` hard-codes only `HUMAN` and `QUAD` (the latter including a **5-joint unbranched tail chain**, which *is* the right axial primitive, and a bounded edit to one file).

**(3) The correspondence signal is weakest exactly where you need it.** DINOv2 features on a low-contrast grey-brown flank in turbid green water are far weaker than on a patterned housecat in a living room, and your own prior pass found DINOv2 underperforming on shark imagery (corroborated by the DS@GT +0.03 vs +0.13 result). The other preprocessing dependencies degrade the same way: optical flow through backscatter and caustics, monocular depth badly out-of-distribution with no ground plane and no scale cues.

**(4) 3C gives you the wrong output object.** lab4d/BANMo produce a learned canonical 3D *feature field*, not an inspectable 2D pigmentation map a biologist can audit by eye. You would still need a UV stage and a proof that it is consistent across individuals.

**(5) Free-deformation canonical spaces are not identity coordinates.** Nerfies/HyperNeRF-style canonical spaces are fixed only up to the deformation field's gauge — two independently optimised sightings land in *different* canonical spaces and their textures are not comparable. Your existing forward-LBS, skeleton-anchored design is the right choice and should not be swapped for a deformation-field NeRF.

**(6) 3A may already be good enough, which is a failure mode for the *proposal*, not for the lab.** If a self-supervised image-space unwrapper matches or beats the full template pipeline, the honest conclusion is that the 3D template was unnecessary. Plan to report that.

### Kill criterion

**3A:** if monocular surface-normal estimation on ≥100 real sevengill frames produces normals that a human cannot recognise as a shark's body surface — no coherent dorsal/lateral separation — the guidance signal is absent and 3A is dead. **One day of work, and it gates the entire pelage-unwrapping route.**

Calibrate against 3DInvarReID's +7.9 (best case, humans, richest possible supervision) — **≥3 points is already an optimistic ask.**

A sevengill transiting past a diver gives a narrow baseline, no orbit, self-occluding lateral undulation, and SfM that will not solve. ## The honest answer to the headline question

Nobody has ever done canonical-3D-surface re-identification on a fish, a shark, or any elongate or limbless animal, and the negative held up under three adversarial passes plus a code-level sweep: the complete DensePose CSE registry is **thirteen meshes — one human and twelve placental mammals, zero fish** [PRIMARY]; A-CSM's shipped articulation is 3–7 rigid parts; released fish and shark computer vision is entirely 2D keypoints and 2D embeddings [PRIMARY, repo sweep]. So doing it for *Notorynchus cepedianus* would be genuinely first-of-kind. But novelty is not the same as being right, and four things should temper it. **First, the measured payoff is small even where every advantage exists** — +7.9 Rank-1 in the one real human 3D ablation, +1.99 for explicit 3D on Market-1501, ~0 for 3D frontalization on faces, and the only published animal measurement (+8.8 on Grevy's zebra) sits on a dataset a generic 2D baseline already scores 1.0000 on. **Second, the field routes around the problem and it works**: whale-shark ID crops a small region posterior to the gill slits; the salmon system anchors patches to the lateral line and gets 0.609 → 0.860 cross-camera mAP with no 3D at all; the *existing sevengill programme* has been matching individuals in La Jolla since 2010 on the nares-to-gill freckle patch — **the most rigid, least-bending region of the animal**. **Third, the isometry assumption the whole idea silently rested on is false**: the fish literature states that the greatest bending strain occurs *nearest the skin*, sonomicrometry in a leopard shark at a leisurely 1 BL/s gives ±3.9–6.6% longitudinal strain, shark skin is *most* extensible in exactly the direction arc-length runs, and its stiffness is actively modulated by swimming speed — so the flattened chart is approximately, not exactly, bend-invariant, the error is antisymmetric between the flanks, and it is not even a pure function of pose. **Fourth, and most important: the premise is still unverified.** There is no published measurement that sevengill speckling is stable through growth, the nearest analogue (epaulette sharks) found juvenile and neonate patterns *unreliable*, and sevengills expand several-fold in length before maturity in nurseries that a California corpus will over-sample.

Against all that, one genuine and non-obvious advantage survives, and it is the reason to keep Approach 2 alive rather than kill it now: **medicine has shipped this exact operation on bent tubes for twenty-five years, in production, under BSD and MIT**, and the specific design that makes it work — arc-length measured on the *centerline* rather than integrated along the skin, circumferential angle carried by a rotation-minimizing frame rather than a Frenet frame, branch insertions harmonically stretched, the chart anchored to ordered anatomical contours — transfers to a shark almost unchanged, and is *strictly better posed* here than in medicine because you have a rest-pose template and can compute the chart once, offline, then carry it through skinning. That is a real contribution and it costs a few weeks to test. But it is the *second* experiment. The first is four weeks on Melops, and it will tell you whether the identity signal lives in the deformable body at all — because if it lives in the head and gills, as a decade of sevengill field practice suggests, then the elegant thing to build is not a canonical surface. It is a very good rigid-patch matcher, a species column, and a partnership with the people who already have the photographs.
