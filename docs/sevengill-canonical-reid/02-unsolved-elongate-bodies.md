## (b) What is unsolved for elongate, bending bodies

**Reframe: the tube is already solved, and not by us.** A centreline-arc-length × circumferential-angle chart on a genus-0 tube is shipped, twenty-five-year-old clinical technology. VMTK carries exactly this pair — an `AbscissaMetric` "computed from the curvilinear abscissa defined on the centerlines" and an `AngularMetric` spanning (−π, +π) — under BSD (Izzo, Steinman, Manini & Antiga, JOSS 2018) [PRIMARY]; conformal virtual colonoscopy flattens the lumen to a rectangle so every surface point is seen exactly once (Haker, Angenent, Tannenbaum & Kikinis, IEEE TMI 2000; Hong & Gu, ACM SPM 2006). Two details settle bend-invariance in our favour. VMTK rejects the Frenet frame for parallel transport so that "no artificial torsion is introduced" [PRIMARY] — a Frenet normal flips sign at a curvature inflection, which a swimming shark crosses every tailbeat, whereas a rotation-minimising frame accrues *zero net rotation under planar bending*. And arc length is measured on the **medial axis** and pushed outward, not integrated on the skin; the neutral axis of a bent beam is inextensional to first order, so *s* survives bending by construction. TubULAR, the closest prior work, concedes the converse in its README — its chart is "surface Lagrangian" because "deformation of the surface in 3D changes the mapping to the plane" — precisely because it integrates *s* along the *surface* (Mitchell & Cislo, Nature Methods 2023) [PRIMARY]. Better: compute the chart **once, offline, on the rest-pose template** and carry it to posed instances through the existing LBS bijection. Medicine cannot do that — organs have no rest pose — and a shark does.

The open problems are therefore three, and sharper than "how do you flatten a tube."

**(i) Fins break both the atlas and the skinning.** Conformal maps incur near-exponential area distortion in long thin protrusions: a fin-bearing shark either crushes its fins to zero area or blows up the trunk. Three borrowed exits exist — VMTK branch decomposition, whose harmonic `vmtkbranchmapping` "stretches the longitudinal metric to correctly account for the presence of insertion regions at bifurcations" [PRIMARY]; automatic cone singularities for minimal area distortion (Sawhney & Crane, *Boundary First Flattening*, ACM TOG; MIT); or excluding fins from the identity surface. **The corpus is silent on all three for any animal.** The third pays a bonus: a fin-free trunk chart is indifferent to *Notorynchus* having one dorsal fin, dissolving rather than patching the 12.5 % of `config/keypoints_16.yaml` undefined here (ids 10–11, `second_dorsal_*`) alongside id 3, hardcoded to "Back edge of last (5th) gill slit" for a seven-gilled animal. The peduncle is harder: it compresses to a keel, the cross-section stops being star-shaped about the medial axis, and a ray at angle φ meets the surface twice — the colon literature's "obstruction problem."

**(ii) Isometry is a load-bearing assumption nobody has measured on a *swimming* shark.** That absence is itself a first-class finding. Shark skin is a pressurised, cross-helically fibred exotendon: subdermal pressure varies with swimming speed, skin stress varies with pressure, and skin stress controls skin *stiffness* (Wainwright, Vosburgh & Hebrank, Science 202:747–749, 1978) — contested in print (Hebrank & Hebrank, Biol. Bull. 171(1)), so cite as mechanism, not settled fact. Fibre angles run 45–65° and the skin is *more extensible longitudinally* than dorsoventrally (Hagood et al., Integr. Comp. Biol. 63(6):1154, 2023) — maximally compliant in exactly the direction *s* runs, and speed-modulated, so a learned correction cannot be a pure function of pose. The one hard number is indirect — leopard-shark red-muscle strain at only ~1.0 BL/s is ±3.9 % anterior, ±6.6 % mid-body, ±4.8 % posterior, in phase with local midline curvature and reproduced by a bending-beam model (Donley & Shadwick, JEB 206(7):1117, 2003) — and in fishes the strain gradient is greatest *near the skin* (Sci. Rep. 11, s41598-021-88828-x, 2021). **Derived by us, not published:** strain scales with distance from the neutral axis, so skin strain is ≥ the muscle value and antisymmetric — convex flank stretching while the concave compresses in the same frame — and 5 % over a ~1.5 m trunk displaces flank features ~7.5 cm, many scar diameters. The *coordinate* is bend-invariant; the *metric* is not. Colonography sets the honest expectation: 96.0/96.1 % ordered-fold matching still required nonrigid B-spline registration *on the flattened domain* (haustral fold matching, Med. Image Anal. c.2013). Flatten, then nonrigidly register; never rigidly compare. **No *in vivo* skin-strain measurement during swimming exists for any shark — ex vivo uniaxial tensile data does (Naresh, Arumugam & Sanjeevi 1997, ultimate stress 24–32 MPa in four directions), which is exactly what makes the in vivo gap conspicuous and no swimming kinematics exist for any hexanchiform.** "More anguilliform than lamnids" is a morphological expectation bracketed by measured subcarangiform sharks (Webb & Keyes, Fishery Bulletin 1982; Berio et al., JFB 2025) and the eel end of the continuum (0.58 BL wavelength vs 1.00 for trout; Di Santo et al., PNAS 2021) — and located inside that interval by nothing.

**(iii) Nobody has done this on a moving, free-swimming, uncalibrated subject.** Every method above assumes a segmented volume of a stationary organ; we have monocular RGB in 3–6 m visibility. Template-space charting is the escape hatch — chart stability becomes exact by construction, confining residual error to fitting error plus (ii).

**Articulation — the constructive counterpoint.** A medial axis cannot be represented by `core/skeleton.py`, which parents nearly everything to `body_midpoint_dorsal`, leaving ~2 lateral-bending DOF (`gill_slit` yaw, `caudal_notch` yaw) for the whole animal. The fix is not more DOF but *the right basis*: four eigenworm modes capture >95 % of *C. elegans* posture variance (Stephens, Johnson-Kerner, Bialek & Ryu, PLoS Comput. Biol. 4:e1000028, 2008; independently restated by Broekmans et al., eLife 5:e17227, 2016); three PCs exceed 90 % of teleost-larva tail-shape variance (eLife reviewed preprint 98347). So: a serial midline chain of 12–20 segments, PCA'd to 4–6 modes. Broekmans is also the warning — the basis broke at large-amplitude *self-occluding* bends, exactly what a tail crossing the trunk in kelp produces. The LBS code is reusable unchanged: colon unfolding by skeletal subspace deformation drives the centreline straight and carries the lumen mesh as skin (MICCAI 2008) — our own code path run backwards.

**Annotation bootstrap — the hardest blocker, but cheaper than we assumed.** A human cannot reliably point at "the same spot" on a featureless flank. DensePose bought its way out with 50K annotated humans and >5M manual correspondences (Güler, Neverova & Kokkinos, CVPR 2018). Two routes genuinely reduce that. CSE replaces the hand-authored chart+UV atlas with learned per-vertex embeddings — no atlas to author — but its registry holds exactly 13 canonical meshes, one human and twelve mammals, zero non-tetrapods (Neverova et al., NeurIPS 2020) [PRIMARY]. Transfer to proximal classes cut chimp annotation to ~3 points over 930 instances / 654 images, but from a *human* donor, and the title says **proximal** (Sanakoyeu et al., CVPR 2020); a hexanchiform has no proximal donor. A-CSM trains from masks with keypoints optional (Kulkarni et al., CVPR 2020; Apache-2.0) but ships 7 parts for horse and 3 for bird — the same star topology we are escaping, so it is not the cure for our skeleton. The cheap route is anatomy: seven gill slits, dorsal insertion, pelvics, cloaca and caudal keel form an *ordered antero-posterior sequence* of iso-*s* contours, which a landmark-driven quasiconformal map consumes directly (MICCAI 2014). A handful of real constraints, not 91 — `shark_smpl.py:109–111` builds its "91 dense surface landmarks" by `torch.linspace(0, num_verts-1, 91)`, and evenly spaced vertex indices are not correspondence.

**Falsifiable milestones.**
1. **Chart existence (one afternoon).** Extract a maximal-inscribed-sphere centreline from the 3912-vertex template in SlicerVMTK. If the medial axis wanders into the fins or caudal lobe instead of tracking the vertebral column, the premise fails before any modelling spend.
2. **Isometry budget.** Pose the template across the bracketed curvature range and measure inter-mark geodesic drift in (s, φ). Drift beyond the descriptor's matching tolerance invalidates rigid comparison in chart space and mandates the nonrigid layer. Publish the number — none exists for any shark.
3. **Beat the incumbent, not a strawman.** On matched splits, exceed HotSpotter/MiewID on the lateral head-and-gill freckle patch — the region both operating sevengill programmes already use, and the region that barely bends — and self-supervised image-space pelage unwrapping, which needs no template and no 3D (arXiv:2506.15369, 2025). The floor: MegaDescriptor-L-384 scores 62.02 % on WhaleSharkID (aquatic rows 69.25 % vs terrestrial 84.59 %, both derived by us from the per-dataset CSV).
4. **Annotatability.** Show above-chance inter-annotator agreement on the ordered iso-*s* sequence (gill slits → dorsal insertion → pelvics → cloaca → keel) over ~500 instances. Failure kills the CSE route, leaving mask-only A-CSM or a fin-free template-space chart. Prerequisite: SharkScarAnnotator has **no species column**, so it cannot store a sevengill annotation at all.
